01 / The question

Access should not depend on a person's ZIP code or paycheck.

Millions of people in the United States face delayed care because providers are unavailable, treatment is unaffordable, or systems are difficult to navigate. These pressures fall hardest on rural communities, low-income families, and people already experiencing discrimination.

Thesis

Access to necessary healthcare is connected to a person's ability to live with dignity and receive the care they need. Systemic healthcare barriers, physician shortages, and high costs in the U.S. disproportionately harm communities; applying Catholic Social Teaching, particularly the principle of Human Dignity, provides a moral foundation for policy reforms that ensure equitable, affordable, and universal healthcare access.

Key barriers

  • Physician shortages make appointments and specialized care difficult to access, especially in rural communities.
  • High premiums, deductibles, and prescription costs force many patients to delay treatment.
  • Transportation, language, disability, discrimination, and complex systems create additional barriers.
